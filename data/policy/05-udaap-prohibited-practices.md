# Policy: Unfair, Deceptive, or Abusive Acts or Practices (UDAAP)

**Applies to:** Every customer-facing communication, including every drafted complaint response. This is a cross-cutting policy, not limited to a specific complaint type.

## Requirements
A statement, practice, or omission may violate UDAAP if it is:
- **Unfair** — causes substantial injury the customer could not reasonably avoid, not outweighed by a countervailing benefit.
- **Deceptive** — a representation, omission, or practice likely to mislead a reasonable consumer, and material to their decision.
- **Abusive** — materially interferes with a consumer's ability to understand a term or condition, or takes unreasonable advantage of a consumer's lack of understanding, inability to protect their interests, or reasonable reliance on us to act in their interest.

## What this means for a drafted response
- Never state or imply a remedy, outcome, or timeline that is not actually authorized or accurate ("your fee has been waived" before it is actually processed).
- Never omit a material fact the customer would reasonably need to understand their situation (e.g., omitting that a "waiver" applies only to this billing cycle, not going forward).
- Never use language that could be read as pressuring a customer to withdraw a complaint or forgo a right (e.g., "we've resolved this, so there's no need to pursue it further with any other agency").
- Avoid technical or legal jargon the customer could not reasonably be expected to understand without explanation.

## What an analyst or agent may say
- May state exactly what remedy has been approved and when it will take effect.
- May NOT use absolute or guarantee language ("this will never happen again," "you are fully protected") beyond what is actually true and verifiable.

## Escalation trigger
Any complaint suggesting the customer was misled by marketing, loan terms, or a prior company representative's statements should be escalated — this is a potential UDAAP exposure, not a routine service issue.

## Remedy authority
Not applicable directly — this policy governs *how* any remedy is communicated, under every other policy in this set.

*Source: Dodd-Frank Act §§ 1031, 1036 (unfair/deceptive/abusive standard); FTC Act § 5 (unfair/deceptive standard, pre-dating Dodd-Frank). Generic, illustrative summary for internal training/demo purposes, not legal advice.*
