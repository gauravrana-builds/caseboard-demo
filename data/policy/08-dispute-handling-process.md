# Internal Policy: General Dispute Handling Process

**Applies to:** Any complaint not governed more specifically by another policy in this set (i.e., the default process).

## Standard process
1. **Acknowledge** receipt within 3 business days.
2. **Investigate** — compare the customer's claim against account and transaction records.
3. **Classify** the outcome as one of: confirmed (company error), not confirmed (no error found), partially confirmed (mixed), or unable to verify (insufficient data).
4. **Respond** in writing within 15 calendar days of receipt (consistent with standard regulatory complaint-handling expectations), addressing every distinct allegation raised in the complaint individually — a response that addresses only the primary complaint and ignores a secondary request is incomplete.
5. **Close** the case with a documented root-cause category for internal trend reporting.

## What counts as a complete response
- Every distinct factual allegation is addressed (confirmed, not confirmed, or unable to verify).
- Every distinct request the customer made is addressed (a refund request and an account-closure request in the same complaint both need a response, even if only one is granted).
- The response states a clear next step or outcome — never ends on an open-ended "we are looking into it" with no indication of what happens next, unless the case is genuinely still pending additional information.

## What an analyst or agent may say
- May state what was investigated and the outcome in plain language.
- May NOT close a case as "resolved" if any raised allegation was not actually addressed.

## Escalation trigger
Use the dedicated Escalation Criteria policy (document 09) for attorney/regulator/vulnerability triggers. This document covers only the baseline process for complaints that don't meet those triggers.

*This is an internal, generic policy created for a training/demonstration environment and does not reflect any specific company's actual policy.*
