# Policy: Credit Reporting Disputes (Fair Credit Reporting Act)

**Applies to:** Any complaint alleging inaccurate, incomplete, or unverifiable information reported to a consumer reporting agency (credit bureau).

## Requirements
- As a furnisher of information, we must investigate a dispute (received either directly from the consumer or relayed by a credit bureau) within **30 days**, extendable to **45 days** if the consumer provides additional information during the 30-day window.
- We must review all relevant information provided by the consumer, correct any inaccuracy found, and report the results of the investigation back to the consumer and, if applicable, to the credit bureau.
- If an item is found to be inaccurate or unverifiable, it must be corrected or deleted from our reporting **promptly**.
- We may not resume reporting an item we previously deleted unless we can certify the information is accurate and complete.
- A consumer may request a statement of the dispute to be included in their credit file if the dispute is not resolved to their satisfaction.

## What an analyst or agent may say
- May confirm whether our internal records match or contradict what is reported (e.g., payment history, account status).
- May state the applicable 30/45-day investigation timeline.
- May NOT promise a specific bureau will update within a specific number of days — we control our own furnishing, not the bureau's processing timeline.

## Escalation trigger
A **third or subsequent dispute on the same item**, or any complaint stating an investigation was closed with no explanation of what was actually checked, should be escalated — repeated unresolved disputes carry elevated regulatory risk.

## Remedy authority
Front-line staff may initiate a correction to our own furnished data (status, balance, payment history) when the internal transaction record clearly supports the correction. Removing an account entirely, or correcting identity-mismatch issues, requires supervisor or fraud-team review.

*Source: Fair Credit Reporting Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1681 et seq. Generic, illustrative summary for internal training/demo purposes, not legal advice.*
