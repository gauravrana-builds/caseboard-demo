# Internal Policy: Escalation Criteria

**Applies to:** Every complaint — this is the first policy that should be checked, before drafting any response.

## Mandatory escalation triggers (no response may be drafted; route directly to legal/compliance)
- Any mention of an attorney, lawyer, legal counsel, or stated intent to pursue legal action.
- Any mention of a regulator (CFPB, state Attorney General, state banking regulator, or any other government agency) being contacted or already involved.
- Any allegation of discrimination on a protected basis.
- Any complaint alleging identity theft or fraud where the customer disputes the debt or account is theirs at all.

## Standard high-risk flags (route to supervisor review before response; may still draft, but hold for sign-off)
- Customer indicates a vulnerability (elderly, cognitive impairment, military service with a PCS or deployment-related hardship, limited English proficiency, stated disability).
- Customer has filed 2 or more complaints on the same issue within the past 6 months.
- Dollar amount in dispute exceeds $500.
- Complaint alleges a pattern (e.g., "this has happened multiple times," "other customers have told me the same thing").

## What this means operationally
- For a **mandatory escalation trigger**: do not draft a customer-facing response. Package the complaint, the relevant facts gathered so far, and the specific trigger reason, and route to legal/compliance.
- For a **standard high-risk flag**: a draft may be prepared, but it requires supervisor sign-off before being sent, in addition to normal review.

## What an analyst or agent may say
- May state clearly and factually why a case has been escalated (which trigger applied).
- May NOT draft any substantive response to the underlying complaint once a mandatory trigger is identified — only the escalation routing itself.

*This is an internal, generic policy created for a training/demonstration environment and does not reflect any specific company's actual policy.*
