# Policy: Debt Collection Conduct (Regulation F / FDCPA)

**Applies to:** Any complaint about collection call frequency, collector communication tactics, or collection timing.

## Requirements
- **Call frequency limit ("7-in-7"):** No more than 7 telephone calls to a consumer regarding a particular debt within a 7-consecutive-day period, and no call within 7 days of a prior telephone conversation about that debt.
- **Time-of-day restriction:** No collection calls before 8:00 a.m. or after 9:00 p.m. in the consumer's local time zone, unless the consumer has given prior consent.
- **Cease-communication requests:** If a consumer requests (verbally or in writing) that collection calls stop, or asks to be contacted only by a specific method (e.g., mail only), that request must be honored going forward.
- **Third-party contact:** Collectors generally may not discuss a debt with a consumer's employer, family, or other third parties, except to confirm location information, and only once.
- **Prohibited conduct:** No threats of legal action not actually intended or authorized, no false representation of the amount or legal status of a debt, no harassing or abusive language.

## What an analyst or agent may say
- May confirm whether the call log shows the number of contacts and whether it exceeds the 7-in-7 threshold.
- May confirm whether a cease-communication or contact-preference request is logged and whether it was honored.
- May NOT characterize a collector's tone or intent — state only what the log objectively shows (number and timing of calls).

## Escalation trigger
Any complaint alleging call-frequency violations, workplace contact, threats, or failure to honor a cease-communication request must be escalated to compliance — these are direct regulatory violations if substantiated, not routine customer service issues.

## Remedy authority
No fee waiver applies to a conduct complaint. The correct response is an acknowledgment, a corrective action on the account (e.g., adding a contact-preference flag), and escalation where conduct rules were violated.

*Source: Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1692 et seq.; Regulation F, 12 C.F.R. Part 1006. Generic, illustrative summary for internal training/demo purposes, not legal advice.*
