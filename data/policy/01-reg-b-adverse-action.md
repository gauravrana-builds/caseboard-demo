# Policy: Adverse Action Notices (Regulation B / ECOA)

**Applies to:** Any complaint involving loan denial, a change in credit terms, or a reduction in credit limit.

## Requirements
- Equal Credit Opportunity Act (ECOA) and its implementing Regulation B require written notice within **30 days** of taking adverse action on a credit application, or within 30 days of an adverse action on an existing account.
- The notice must state **specific, principal reasons** for the adverse action (e.g., "insufficient income," "high debt-to-income ratio") — generic or vague reasons ("did not meet our criteria") do not satisfy the requirement.
- The customer has the right to request the specific reasons within 60 days if not already provided.
- Reasons stated must match the actual factors used in the credit decision (whether a model-based score or manual underwriting). Never state a reason that was not actually a basis for the decision.

## What an analyst or agent may say
- May confirm the specific denial reason(s) on file and restate them in plain language.
- May NOT reinterpret, soften, or add reasons beyond what the underwriting record shows.
- May NOT state or imply the decision will be reversed unless a documented underwriting override has occurred.

## Escalation trigger
Any complaint disputing the *accuracy* of a stated adverse action reason, or alleging discriminatory treatment, must be escalated to compliance/legal — this is outside normal complaint-response authority.

## Remedy authority
None at the front-line level. Adverse action disputes are not fee-waiver or goodwill-credit situations; they require underwriting review.

*Source: Equal Credit Opportunity Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1691 et seq.; Regulation B, 12 C.F.R. Part 1002. This is a generic, illustrative summary for internal training/demo purposes, not legal advice.*
